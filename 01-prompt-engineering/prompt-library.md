# Prompt Library

## Selected Workplace Scenario

**Role:** Digital Solutions Analyst

These prompts are designed to support common tasks in digital projects, including communication, information processing, action tracking, and project planning.

---

## 1. Deadline-Shift Email

**Workplace Task:** Communicating a project deadline change  
**Framework:** C.A.R.E.

**Prompt:**

Context: Our project deadline has been delayed by two weeks, from May 30 to June 13.

Audience: A six-person project team.

Request: Write a professional and concise email explaining the schedule change and its impact on the project. Ask team members to update their timelines and confirm any issues by Thursday.

Expectations: Keep the email clear, professional, concise, and action-oriented. Do not invent any additional information.

**Expected Output:**

A concise professional email explaining the deadline change and the required team actions.

**When to use:** When a project timeline changes and the team needs clear communication.

---

## 2. Meeting Notes to Action Items

**Workplace Task:** Extracting project actions  
**Framework:** C.A.R.E.

**Prompt:**

Context: I have unstructured notes from a digital project meeting.

Audience: The project team.

Request: Extract all confirmed action items from the notes.

Expectations: Present the result as a table with Action Item, Owner, Deadline, Priority, and Status. If an owner or deadline is not mentioned, write [Not Specified]. Do not guess or invent missing information.

**Expected Output:**

| Action Item | Owner | Deadline | Priority | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Action] | [Name / Not Specified] | [Date / Not Specified] | [High/Medium/Low] | [Status] |

**When to use:** After meetings when project actions need to be organized and tracked.

---

## 3. Decision-Ready Project Summary

**Workplace Task:** Summarizing project information  
**Framework:** C.A.R.E.

**Prompt:**

Context: I have project updates containing decisions, risks, open issues, and unresolved questions.

Audience: A project manager who needs a quick decision-ready overview.

Request: Summarize the information and organize it into Key Decisions, Risks, Open Issues, Actions, and Missing Information.

Expectations: Put the most important information first, preserve uncertainty, and clearly mark information that is not specified. Do not turn assumptions into confirmed facts.

**Expected Output:**

- Key Decisions
- Top Risks
- Open Issues
- Action Items
- Missing Information

**When to use:** When project information is lengthy or unstructured and needs to support a decision.

## 4. Professional Project Update

**Workplace Task:** Writing a project update  
**Framework:** C.A.R.E.

**Prompt:**

Context: I need to communicate the current status of a digital project to stakeholders.

Audience: Project stakeholders.

Request: Write a short professional project update covering progress, current risks, open issues, and next steps.

Expectations: Use a professional and neutral tone. Keep the message concise. Separate confirmed information from unresolved issues and do not invent missing details.

**Expected Output:**

A short stakeholder-ready project update with clear sections for status, risks, open issues, and next steps.

**When to use:** For regular project status communication.

---

## 5. Project Planning Prompt

**Workplace Task:** Planning a digital project  
**Framework:** R.C.T.O.

**Prompt:**

Role: Act as a project planning assistant supporting a Digital Solutions Analyst.

Context: I need to organize a digital project around one clear business goal.

Task: Create a concise plan using Goal → Mechanisms → Phases → Tasks. Include dependencies, risks, owners, and deadlines when they are provided.

Output: Present the plan as a structured table. Mark unknown owners or deadlines as [Not Specified]. Do not invent information.

**Expected Output:**

A concise project plan showing the goal, mechanisms, phases, tasks, dependencies, risks, owners, and deadlines.

**When to use:** At the beginning of a project or when an existing plan needs structure.

---

## 6. Before-Launch Risk Review

**Workplace Task:** Reviewing launch readiness  
**Framework:** C.A.R.E.

**Prompt:**

Context: A digital product is approaching launch and project information includes technical issues, marketing readiness, deadlines, and unresolved decisions.

Audience: The project manager and project team.

Request: Identify the main launch risks, dependencies, unresolved decisions, and actions required before launch.

Expectations: Prioritize the most important risks, distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions, and flag missing information instead of guessing.

**Expected Output:**

A prioritized launch-readiness summary containing Risks, Dependencies, Open Decisions, Required Actions, and Missing Information.

**When to use:** Before a product or digital service launch.

---

## 7. Stakeholder Follow-Up

**Workplace Task:** Following up on an unanswered request  
**Framework:** C.A.R.E.

**Prompt:**

Context: A stakeholder has requested information about a digital project and has not yet received a response.

Audience: The stakeholder.

Request: Draft a polite and concise follow-up message requesting the required information or response.

Expectations: Keep the tone professional and respectful. Clearly state what is needed and avoid adding information that was not provided.

**Expected Output:**

A short professional follow-up message with a clear request and next step.

**When to use:** When a stakeholder request needs a timely follow-up.

---

## Summary

These prompts demonstrate how structured prompting can support communication, information processing, planning, risk review, and stakeholder coordination in a Digital Solutions Analyst role.
